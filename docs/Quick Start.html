Carl-Cam

A live desktop camera with three graphic looks, a hand-shaped picture frame and personal gesture controls.

This is a development build. There is no pretrained gesture demo yet. Real-person gesture checks, live camera/OBS behavior and clean-Windows installation still require testing. If a check fails, use mouse and keyboard while keeping your recordings for a retry.

MediaPipe usage metrics: camera processing and training run locally. MediaPipe may send performance and usage metrics to Google; its input images and video are not sent to Google. Carl-Cam does not save webcam photos/video or upload numerical training examples. This development build still requires native, hardware, and installer verification before public release.

Install and open

  1. Run the Carl-Cam Windows installer and choose where to install it. No Python or commands are needed.
  2. Open Carl-Cam from the Start menu. The camera and outgoing virtual camera stay off until you start them.
  3. Follow setup: read the privacy explanation, select and test your camera, choose personal training or mouse/keyboard, then read the optional virtual-camera instructions.

The development installer is unsigned. Only use a build from a source you trust; do not disable Windows security to run it. An unsigned file is not proof of a safe application.

Try all three looks without training

Start your selected camera, choose Red, Yellow or Blue, and choose Mouse frame. Place four corners around the part of the camera view you want to filter. Only the inside changes; your camera image is not stretched into that shape. Choose Clear frame for the plain camera.

Red uses graphic black/cream shapes with red paper energy. Yellow uses flat bright tones, rounded pop-art forms and pink accents. Blue uses cool tones and translucent angular panels. They deliberately look illustrated rather than natural.

Train your gestures

If processing stays slow for five seconds, Carl-Cam may request a smaller camera input. It waits at least ten seconds before another reduction and shows actual dimensions below the preview. The picture frame and final canvas stay the same size. Choose Try higher input quality to request the preferred size again; quality never rises automatically. Some camera drivers ignore unsupported sizes. Hand commands pause during the change—form a fresh pose afterward. Lower input quality is a fallback, not a 30-fps guarantee.

Choose Train my gestures. Before recording, assign thumbs-up, open palm and “I love you” one-to-one to the three filters. The frame pose and peace-sign menu pose have fixed functions.

  1. Agree to save numerical examples. Carl-Cam saves measurements, labels and recording identifiers, not photos or video.
  2. Follow each drawing and prompt. There are five rounds for each of five poses plus ordinary movement: 30 rounds in one workflow. Positive rounds need 12 usable seconds; ordinary-movement rounds need 15. Invalid/stale observations do not count, so actual time can be longer.
  3. Vary position, angle and distance as prompted. A requested lighting change may be skipped. Keep both hands visible for the frame pose; unsupported new poses cannot be added.
  4. Review your recordings, train the neural candidates and follow the commands-disabled comparison. This exercise does not change your active filter.
  5. Complete the held-out check and fresh activation rehearsal. Explicitly activate only after all five commands and ordinary movement pass. A previous working model remains available if a replacement fails.

You may stop and resume through the training screen. Completed rounds remain; an interrupted round restarts. Revealed final checks are not reused for model tuning—reopening that stage asks for fresh examples and checks.

Train on NVIDIA GPU is optional on the recording-review screen. Leave it unchecked for CPU training, or select it to fit the neural candidates on a compatible NVIDIA GPU. Carl-Cam includes the training runtime; no Python or CUDA-toolkit installation is needed. The explanation beside the option shows whether a compatible GPU and driver are available. If GPU training fails, the app reports its CPU retry instead of claiming a GPU fit. The small gesture networks are not guaranteed to train faster on GPU. Finished models are saved for CPU use, so ordinary gesture recognition and later model loading do not require GPU training.

Repeated nearly identical examples may pause the usable-time counter. Follow the position, angle and distance prompts so your recordings cover more than one stationary pose. If hand-depth measurements are unreliable, retry the affected recordings or explicitly continue with the two approved no-depth feature sets. This changes which neural candidates are compared, not the five supported commands.

Frame and switch with your hands

Hold two opposing L-shaped hands in view. Their thumb/palm junctions supply opposite corners; thumb and index directions intersect to make the other corners. Carl-Cam follows this live quadrilateral. Tracking loss stops unsafe commands and eventually removes the frame.

Two switch paths are available: open the head-following menu with the peace sign, move its pointer and pinch to confirm; or release the frame, hold a filter’s assigned direct pose, then re-form the frame. Hovering a choice or beginning a pinch freezes menu movement until the interaction ends.

Mouse and keyboard remain available. Use Tab to focus buttons and Space/Enter to activate one. The custom X closes Carl-Cam; Back to camera returns from a private screen without exiting.

Setup offers optional live control practice; you can skip it or start it later from Help with your camera running. It guides you through framing, all three direct selections and the menu path. With gestures disabled, the same practice uses mouse/keyboard controls. Practice saves no recordings and is not an accuracy test.

If your face leaves too little safe space for the menu, Carl-Cam asks you to move farther from the camera instead of covering your face with choices. The desktop Selected filter and Back to camera controls remain available.

Choose the masking processor

CPU is selected by default and works without an NVIDIA GPU. To try the bundled experimental GPU component, open Settings → Camera, choose NVIDIA GPU — experimental, with CPU fallback, then save. Stop and restart the camera to apply it. Initial startup can take several seconds while bundled files are checked and the GPU initializes. This option accelerates hair, skin and clothing masking; it does not replace your personal gesture model.

You do not need Python or a CUDA toolkit. The GPU option needs a compatible NVIDIA card and driver. If the component cannot start or stops responding, Carl-Cam shows its CPU fallback and keeps the camera controls available. This experimental option does not guarantee 30 frames per second.

Optional outgoing video

Install OBS and its virtual-camera support separately for outgoing video. This installer does not install or launch OBS. Start your physical camera in Carl-Cam, then explicitly choose Start virtual camera. Select OBS Virtual Camera in the receiving application.

Output is unmirrored. The live performance menu appears with upright labels in Menu mode; desktop controls, landmarks, inspection panels and training prompts stay private. Stale source images become black. Output is off after every app launch. A missing/busy driver leaves desktop preview usable; stop a competing sender and retry. A real OBS-driver session has not yet been verified.

Optional classifier comparison

With a tested personal model selected and a camera running, open Inspection and choose Compare with MediaPipe (optional). Neither classifier executes commands or changes your model in this screen. Five attempts each at peace, thumbs-up, open palm and “I love you,” plus one minute of ordinary movement, tune the two systems separately. The same sequence is then repeated with new attempts and fixed thresholds for the untouched test.

Take both hands out of view to release between attempts, then prepare the requested one-hand pose. Click Pose ready or press Space when it is formed. The results show successes/misses, confusion and missing-hand outcomes, accidental commands and timing. The two-hand picture frame has no canned MediaPipe equivalent and is counted separately. These are preliminary results; scores are not calibrated probabilities and a tie or MediaPipe win is reported honestly.

The numerical-save checkbox starts off. Leave it off to discard results on closing, or explicitly enable it before starting to retain categories, scores, frame IDs and the report locally. No images, video or landmarks enter that report. Delete recordings or Reset all local data removes retained comparison results.

Privacy and recovery

Settings changes assignments without relabeling recordings, adjusts hold time, enables reduced motion/high contrast and selects previously tested personal models. Numerical recordings and models are separate from installed program files.

Separately confirmed delete controls remove recordings, personal models or both. Uninstall preserves these items so an update does not erase them. Creator-candidate export contains model artifacts without recordings and does not approve a public demo.

Reset all local data requires confirmation, stops capture/output and removes Carl-Cam’s numerical recordings, models, settings and small local diagnostic logs. It returns to setup and cannot be undone in the app. These logs contain only supported error codes and timestamps, never camera images, landmarks, device names or error-message text.

After selecting a different physical camera, a saved personal model stays disabled for that camera until you complete the guided commands-disabled recheck and explicitly confirm it. The check evaluates the unchanged model; it neither retrains it nor records a new training dataset. Mouse/keyboard stays usable while it is pending.

If something is not working

  • No camera: check Windows camera privacy permission, close another app using that device, refresh and retry. OBS Virtual Camera is not a physical input.
  • Hands missing or unreliable commands: face the camera, keep hands visible, reduce backlighting and use mouse controls while collecting varied examples.
  • Graphic detail disappears: palette-only styling is the fallback when matching region masks are unavailable. Old masks must not be used on different images.
  • Slow preview: performance tuning remains open; successful startup is not a 30-fps guarantee.
  • Model cannot load: keep saved files, choose another tested version or use mouse/keyboard. Do not install arbitrary external model files.
  • Inspect behavior: Inspection shows private intermediate views. Learned commands are disabled there and panels do not enter output.
